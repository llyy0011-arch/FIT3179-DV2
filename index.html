THE BIGGER PICTURE

Beyond the National Average

In 2022, approximately 5.5 million Australians were living with disability, representing 21.4% of the population. However, these national figures do not reveal how disability differs between demographic groups or how access to support varies across Australia.

This story begins with disability prevalence, moves to the geographical distribution of NDIS participants, and then examines barriers to healthcare access among people with disability.

01

THE NATIONAL PICTURE

Who Experiences Disability?

Disability prevalence is not uniform across the Australian population. Age and sex provide two important perspectives for understanding differences that may be hidden within the national average.

DISABILITY PREVALENCE

How Does Disability Differ Across Age Groups?

Australia's overall disability prevalence was 21.4% in 2022. However, prevalence increases substantially with age, with older Australians experiencing much higher rates than younger age groups. The chart also reveals differences between males and females, suggesting that disability prevalence is influenced by both age and sex.

Figure 1. Disability prevalence by age group and sex. Source: ABS, Survey of Disability, Ageing and Carers.

CHANGES BETWEEN 2003 AND 2022

How Has Disability Prevalence Changed Over Time?

Disability prevalence has changed differently across age groups between 2003 and 2022. The dumbbell chart shows that the direction and magnitude of change vary across generations. While some younger age groups experienced increases in disability prevalence, some older age groups recorded declines. This suggests that changes in disability prevalence have not been uniform across the Australian population.

Figure 2. Disability prevalence by age group, 2003 and 2022. Source: ABS.

Understanding who experiences disability is only the beginning. The next question is how disability support is distributed across Australia.

03

ACCESS AND BARRIERS

When Healthcare Is Needed, Can People Access It?

Access to healthcare is a crucial part of living with disability. Yet the need for medical services does not always translate into successful access. Financial and non-financial barriers can prevent people from receiving care.

HEALTHCARE SERVICE ACCESS

How Accessible Are Essential Health Services?

Healthcare service use varies across different age groups and types of care. Seeing a GP for personal health is among the most commonly reported services, with rates exceeding 80% in several age groups. In comparison, hospital admissions are considerably less frequent. This highlights the central role of general practitioners in healthcare for Australians with disability.

Figure 5. Healthcare service use by age group, Australia, 2022. Source: AIHW.

UNMET HEALTHCARE NEEDS

What Prevents People from Seeing a GP?

Waiting times are a major obstacle to accessing GP care among people with disability. Across the population examined, approximately 43.1% reported waiting times as a reason for unmet GP needs, compared with 8.8% who reported cost. Among people with severe or profound disability, waiting times accounted for 41.0%, while cost accounted for 9.0%. These figures suggest that delays in accessing appointments are a more prominent barrier than direct financial costs.

AGE DIFFERENCES

Does Age Change the Experience of GP Access?

Waiting times remain a prominent barrier across age groups, but their relative importance differs by age. They account for approximately 47.7% among people aged 25 to 44 and 47.2% among those aged 45 to 64, compared with 37.0% among people aged 65 and over. Cost-related barriers also differ, reaching 13.9% among those aged 25 to 44, compared with 4.2% among those aged 65 and over. This suggests that younger and middle-aged adults face different challenges from older Australians.

Figure 7. Main reasons for unmet GP needs by age group, Australia, 2022. Source: AIHW.

COST AND GENDER INEQUALITY

Who Is Most Affected by the Cost of GP Care?

Among people with disability aged 25 to 44, 22.6% of females did not see a GP when needed because of cost, compared with 14.2% of males. This represents a gap of 8.4 percentage points.
